# Incident Response Policy
## ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Information Security Management System

### 1. Purpose
This Incident Response Policy defines the requirements for identifying, reporting, 
responding to, and recovering from information security incidents. It ensures incidents 
are managed effectively and consistently in accordance with ISO/IEC 27001:2022.

---

### 2. Scope
This policy applies to:
- All employees, contractors, and third‑party service providers  
- All organisational systems, applications, networks, and cloud services  
- All environments (production, staging, development)  
- All security events that may impact confidentiality, integrity, or availability  

---

### 3. Definitions

#### **Security Event**
Any observable occurrence in a system or network.

#### **Security Incident**
A confirmed or suspected event that compromises confidentiality, integrity, or availability.

#### **Major Incident**
An incident causing significant operational, financial, or reputational impact.

---

### 4. Incident Response Objectives
- Detect incidents quickly  
- Contain and mitigate impact  
- Restore normal operations  
- Preserve evidence for investigation  
- Communicate effectively with stakeholders  
- Prevent recurrence through lessons learned  

---

### 5. Incident Reporting

#### **5.1 Immediate Reporting**
All employees must report suspected or confirmed incidents immediately via:
- Security hotline  
- Incident reporting portal  
- Email to the security team  
- Direct communication with IT/Security  

#### **5.2 Required Information**
Reports should include:
- Description of the incident  
- Systems or data affected  
- Time of detection  
- Actions taken  

---

### 6. Incident Classification
Incidents must be classified as:

- **Low Severity:** Minimal impact, easily contained  
- **Medium Severity:** Noticeable impact requiring coordinated response  
- **High Severity:** Major incident affecting critical systems or sensitive data  

Classification determines escalation and response requirements.

---

### 7. Incident Response Process

#### **7.1 Identification**
- Detect anomalies through monitoring, alerts, or user reports.  
- Validate whether the event is a security incident.

#### **7.2 Containment**
- Isolate affected systems.  
- Block malicious activity.  
- Disable compromised accounts.  

#### **7.3 Eradication**
- Remove malware or malicious code.  
- Patch vulnerabilities.  
- Strengthen controls to prevent recurrence.

#### **7.4 Recovery**
- Restore systems from backups.  
- Validate system integrity.  
- Monitor for residual issues.

#### **7.5 Post‑Incident Review**
- Conduct root cause analysis.  
- Document lessons learned.  
- Update controls, policies, or procedures.  

---

### 8. Communication & Escalation

- High‑severity incidents must be escalated to senior management immediately.  
- External communication (customers, regulators, media) must be approved by management.  
- Third‑party providers must notify the organisation of incidents affecting shared systems.  

---

### 9. Evidence Preservation

- Logs, files, and artifacts must be preserved securely.  
- Chain‑of‑custody procedures must be followed for forensic investigations.  
- Evidence must not be altered or deleted.

---

### 10. Roles & Responsibilities

#### **Security Team**
- Lead incident response activities  
- Coordinate containment, eradication, and recovery  
- Maintain incident response documentation  

#### **IT Team**
- Support technical containment and recovery  
- Provide system access and logs  

#### **Employees**
- Report incidents immediately  
- Follow instructions during response activities  

#### **Senior Management**
- Approve major incident communication  
- Allocate resources for response and recovery  

---

### 11. Testing & Training
- Incident response procedures must be tested annually.  
- Employees must receive incident reporting training.  
- Tabletop exercises must be conducted for high‑risk scenarios.

---

### 12. Review
This policy will be reviewed annually or after major incidents.

---

### 13. Approval
Approved by senior management as part of the ISMS governance framework.
