# Change Management Policy
## ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Information Security Management System

### 1. Purpose
This Change Management Policy defines the requirements for planning, approving, 
implementing, and reviewing changes to systems, applications, infrastructure, and 
processes. It ensures changes are controlled, documented, and do not introduce 
unacceptable risks, in alignment with ISO/IEC 27001:2022.

---

### 2. Scope
This policy applies to:
- All IT systems, applications, services, and infrastructure  
- All cloud environments, CI/CD pipelines, and configurations  
- All employees, contractors, and third‑party providers involved in change activities  
- All changes that may impact confidentiality, integrity, or availability  

---

### 3. Types of Changes
Changes must be classified as:

#### **Standard Changes**
- Pre‑approved, low‑risk, repeatable changes  
- Executed according to documented procedures  

#### **Normal Changes**
- Changes requiring assessment, approval, testing, and scheduling  
- Includes updates, patches, configuration changes, and deployments  

#### **Emergency Changes**
- Changes required to resolve critical incidents or vulnerabilities  
- Must follow expedited approval and post‑implementation review  

---

### 4. Change Management Requirements

#### **4.1 Change Requests**
All changes must be formally requested and documented, including:
- Description of the change  
- Reason and business justification  
- Systems and assets affected  
- Risk assessment  
- Testing requirements  
- Rollback plan  
- Implementation schedule  

#### **4.2 Risk Assessment**
Changes must be evaluated for:
- Security impact  
- Operational impact  
- Compliance requirements  
- Dependencies and potential conflicts  

High‑risk changes require senior management approval.

---

### 5. Approval Process
- Standard changes follow predefined approval workflows.  
- Normal changes require approval from the Change Advisory Board (CAB) or designated approvers.  
- Emergency changes require expedited approval from IT/Security leadership.  

No change may be implemented without documented approval.

---

### 6. Testing Requirements
Changes must be tested to ensure:
- Functionality  
- Security  
- Compatibility  
- Performance  

Testing must occur in non‑production environments unless justified.

---

### 7. Deployment Requirements
- Deployments must follow documented procedures.  
- Only authorised personnel may implement changes.  
- Deployment windows must minimise business disruption.  
- Monitoring must be enabled during and after deployment.

---

### 8. Rollback Planning
All changes must include a rollback plan that:
- Restores systems to a known good state  
- Minimises downtime  
- Ensures data integrity  

Rollback plans must be tested for high‑risk changes.

---

### 9. Documentation
The following must be recorded:
- Change request  
- Approval evidence  
- Testing results  
- Deployment logs  
- Rollback actions (if used)  
- Post‑implementation review  

Documentation must be retained for audit purposes.

---

### 10. Post‑Implementation Review
Normal and emergency changes must undergo review to:
- Confirm success  
- Identify issues or incidents  
- Update procedures or controls  
- Capture lessons learned  

---

### 11. Emergency Changes
Emergency changes:
- Must be used only when necessary  
- Must be logged and documented  
- Must undergo full review after implementation  
- Must be incorporated into future risk assessments  

---

### 12. Compliance
Non‑compliance may result in:
- Revocation of change privileges  
- Disciplinary action  
- Contract termination (for third parties)  

---

### 13. Review
This policy will be reviewed annually or after major changes to systems or processes.

---

### 14. Approval
Approved by senior management as part of the ISMS governance framework.
