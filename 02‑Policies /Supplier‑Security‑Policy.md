# Supplier Security Policy
## ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Information Security Management System

### 1. Purpose
This Supplier Security Policy defines the requirements for selecting, evaluating, 
managing, and monitoring suppliers and third‑party service providers to ensure they 
meet the organisation’s information security expectations in accordance with 
ISO/IEC 27001:2022.

---

### 2. Scope
This policy applies to:
- All suppliers, contractors, and third‑party service providers  
- All cloud platforms, SaaS applications, and hosted services  
- All external entities processing, storing, or accessing organisational data  
- All employees responsible for supplier management  

---

### 3. Supplier Classification
Suppliers must be classified based on the level of risk they pose:

- **Low Risk:** No access to sensitive systems or data  
- **Medium Risk:** Limited access to internal systems or non‑sensitive data  
- **High Risk:** Access to critical systems, sensitive data, or core operations  

Classification determines the level of security controls required.

---

### 4. Supplier Selection & Due Diligence
Before onboarding, suppliers must undergo security evaluation including:

- Review of security policies and certifications (ISO 27001, SOC 2, etc.)  
- Assessment of data protection practices  
- Evaluation of incident response capabilities  
- Review of contractual and regulatory compliance  
- Risk assessment based on services provided  

High‑risk suppliers require enhanced due diligence.

---

### 5. Contractual Security Requirements
Contracts with suppliers must include:

- Confidentiality and data protection clauses  
- Security control requirements aligned with ISO 27001  
- Breach notification obligations  
- Right to audit or request security evidence  
- Requirements for secure data handling, storage, and disposal  
- Termination and offboarding procedures  

---

### 6. Supplier Access Management
- Supplier access must be approved, documented, and time‑limited.  
- Access must follow least privilege and MFA requirements.  
- Supplier accounts must be monitored and reviewed regularly.  
- Access must be revoked immediately when no longer required.  

---

### 7. Monitoring & Performance Review
Suppliers must be monitored to ensure ongoing compliance:

- Annual security reviews for medium‑ and high‑risk suppliers  
- Review of audit reports, certifications, or security attestations  
- Monitoring of service performance and security incidents  
- Verification of compliance with contractual obligations  

Non‑compliant suppliers must be escalated and remediated.

---

### 8. Supplier Incident Management
- Suppliers must report security incidents promptly.  
- Incidents involving supplier systems must follow the organisation’s incident response process.  
- Joint investigations may be required for shared environments.  
- Lessons learned must be incorporated into risk treatment plans.  

---

### 9. Offboarding & Termination
When a supplier relationship ends:

- Access must be revoked immediately.  
- Data must be returned, deleted, or securely destroyed.  
- Assets must be recovered or sanitised.  
- Contractual obligations must be verified as complete.  

---

### 10. Compliance
Non‑compliance with this policy may result in:
- Suspension or termination of supplier services  
- Contract penalties  
- Legal action  
- Increased monitoring or remediation requirements  

---

### 11. Review
This policy will be reviewed annually or after major changes in supplier relationships or regulatory requirements.

---

### 12. Approval
Approved by senior management as part of the ISMS governance framework.
