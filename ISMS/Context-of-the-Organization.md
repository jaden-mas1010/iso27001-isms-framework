# Context of the Organization
## ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Information Security Management System

### 1. Purpose
This document identifies internal and external issues that influence the organisation’s 
ability to achieve the intended outcomes of the Information Security Management System 
(ISMS). It also defines relevant stakeholders and their expectations.

---

### 2. Internal Issues
The following internal factors affect the ISMS:

- Organisational structure and governance  
- Roles, responsibilities, and decision‑making processes  
- Existing security policies, procedures, and controls  
- Technology stack, infrastructure, and cloud services  
- Internal capabilities, skills, and resource availability  
- Operational processes and workflows  
- Cultural attitudes toward security and compliance  

These internal issues shape how the ISMS is implemented and maintained.

---

### 3. External Issues
The following external factors influence the ISMS:

- Legal and regulatory requirements (e.g., data protection, industry standards)  
- Contractual obligations with customers and suppliers  
- Market conditions and competitive pressures  
- Emerging cybersecurity threats and vulnerabilities  
- Dependence on third‑party service providers and cloud platforms  
- Industry best practices and security frameworks  
- Social, economic, and environmental conditions  

These external issues define the broader environment in which the ISMS operates.

---

### 4. Interested Parties
The organisation has identified the following stakeholders whose needs and expectations 
are relevant to the ISMS:

- Customers and clients  
- Employees and contractors  
- Senior management  
- Regulatory authorities  
- Cloud and technology service providers  
- Suppliers and partners  
- Auditors and assessors  

Each stakeholder group has expectations related to confidentiality, integrity, 
availability, compliance, and risk management.

---

### 5. Needs and Expectations of Interested Parties
Key expectations include:

- Protection of personal and business data  
- Compliance with legal and regulatory requirements  
- Reliable and secure service delivery  
- Effective incident response and communication  
- Assurance of risk‑based security controls  
- Transparency in security practices and governance  

These expectations guide the selection and implementation of Annex A controls.

---

### 6. Strategic Direction
The organisation’s strategic direction supports:

- Strengthening information security governance  
- Reducing risk through preventive and detective controls  
- Ensuring compliance with ISO/IEC 27001:2022  
- Improving resilience and business continuity  
- Enhancing trust with customers and partners  

The ISMS aligns with organisational goals and long‑term security objectives.

---

### 7. Conclusion
Understanding internal and external issues, stakeholder expectations, and strategic 
direction ensures that the ISMS is relevant, effective, and aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022.
